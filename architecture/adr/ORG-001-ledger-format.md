# ORG-001 — Decision ledger format and storage

**Status:** Ratified 2026-04-28 by the operator (Telegram). Amended 2026-05-29 (weekly sharding, T-0148), 2026-08-02 (`executor`, T-2276) and 2026-10-08 (`ts`, `agent` and `id` rules, the operator TG 11474; T-4734).
**HITL:** Ratified.
**Date:** 2026-04-28.
**Justifies via:** P4 (the ledger IS the traceability mechanism), P3 (the schema's `justifies_via` field is the structural enforcement of upward justification).

Build-tier detail (shard layout, the schema as first ratified, the metrics schema, the executor worked case) lives in `notes/2026-10-08-org-001-ledger-reference-relocated.md`. The amendment trail lives in `notes/2026-10-08-org-001-review-history.md`. How to write an entry: `sop/standard/appending-a-ledger-entry.md`.

## Context

P4 ("Traceability is non-negotiable") requires every decision that shaped action to land in a ledger. P3 ("Decisions justify upward") requires every entry to name the principle(s) it serves. Without a concrete schema and storage, both principles are aspirations.

The orchestrator drafted three storage options across Telegram on 2026-04-26/27 (separate `pantheon-ledger` repo vs. `ledger/` directory in `pantheon-config` vs. database). The operator chose option B (directory in `pantheon-config`) on 2026-04-28: principles and the audit chain that justifies-via them stay co-located.

## Decision

**Storage:** `pantheon-config/ledger/` directory, git-tracked.

**Format:** Append-only JSONL, one entry per line, sharded into weekly ISO-week segments (one `.jsonl` per week, each with its own spine). Weekly rather than yearly: at the observed pace (~85 entries/~150 KB per week) a year file under-shards; a week keeps each shard readable while a top-level index keeps cross-week navigation. This is the D10 (minimum traversal to precise information) implementation at the record-file layer. Layout and segment computation: the relocated-reference note.

**Index:** a top-level `INDEX.md` plus a per-segment spine, both generated by the spine groomer. Neither is hand-edited.

**Mutation policy:** Append-only. Entries are never edited or deleted. To correct an error, append a new entry that supersedes the old one (set `supersedes` field to the old `id`). The git history is the immutable backstop, but the in-file convention preserves the audit chain even for readers who never run `git log`.

## Per-entry fields

Every entry carries `id`, `ts`, `agent`, `action`, `rationale` and `justifies_via`. The writer checks for them (see the end of this section). Optional fields: `links` (typed provenance pointers), `refs` (untyped provenance, accepted beside `links`), `cost_usd`, `parent_id`, `supersedes`, `metrics` (close-entries), `executor` and `decided_at`. The per-field reference as first ratified is in the relocated-reference note.

- **`ts` is the write time,** measured in the same command that appends the entry (the operator TG 11474). Write time is monotone with write order, so each entry lands in the right weekly shard and stamps cannot drift forward. A decision made earlier than it was recorded names that time in the optional **`decided_at`** field (ISO 8601 UTC) instead.
- **`id` is `<ts>-<slug>`:** the entry's `ts`, a hyphen, and a short kebab-case slug. It is unique across the ledger.
- **`agent` is the writer's ORG-100 role id** (snake_case, e.g. `head_engineering_orchestrator`, `engineering_orchestrator`, `head_system_orchestrator`, `head_research_orchestrator`, `head_memory_orchestrator`, `head_auditor`), or `human:operator` for an entry the operator writes, including a tool run he performs. A seat with no ORG-100 role (today: the-ancestor) has no `agent` value, so it writes no ledger entries until it has a role. Role ids, not persona names, so the public version of the ledger needs no persona scrub (the operator TG 11474). The vocabulary is ORG-100's; a new role reaches the ledger through ORG-100, not through an amendment here. Values written before 2026-10-08 (`orchestrator`, persona names, `worker:*`, `project:*`) remain valid as history and are mapped to role ids for readers by `agent_role_map`. They are not valid for new entries.
- **`justifies_via`** names at least one principle (`P<n>` for the org, a project prefix for a project, e.g. `echo:WS3`). An empty array is a P4 violation; escalate.
- **`rationale`** is required even when the decision looks obvious: the obvious decays first.

The writer (`ledger_shard`'s shared write-path check) checks these rules for every new entry: it reports a violation now, and refuses one once enforcement is switched on (T-4739). Existing entries are never checked or rewritten.

## Executor attribution

`agent` identifies a **role**, not who acted. When one role has more than one member (e.g. two engineering orchestrators), or one persona has more than one live executor, `agent` alone cannot say who wrote the entry: every reconstruction mechanism (ledger authorship, commit author, reflog, working copy) resolves to the same value, and the chain becomes intact, append-only, and unable to answer the question it exists to answer. P4 is not weakened by a missing entry here; it is weakened by an actor field that has stopped being a key.

The optional **`executor`** field names who acted, as the writer's **seat slot id** (e.g. `engineering-orchestrator-1`, `engineering-orchestrator-2`): a generic slot label, not a persona name, so it distinguishes members of one role without reintroducing what the role-id `agent` keeps out of the public ledger. A session id, pid or unit label may follow where useful. It SHOULD be set whenever the writer's role is shared (more than one seat holds it). Where it is not set and a second executor could plausibly share the role, name the executor in the action or rationale text instead. The field was in use before it was documented (T-2276), and consumers ignore unknown keys, so it is additive. A one-executor-per-identity mutex lowers its urgency; it does not retire it.

## Metrics field (close-entries)

Close-entries (an `action` matching `t<NNNN>_(resolved|landed|closed|done)_*`) may carry a structured `metrics` field with per-cycle data, recorded at close time by the entity emitting the close-entry and never retrofitted. Its purpose: HO can find prior cycles of similar shape before estimating a new one, spot recurring outcome patterns, and quote estimates with empirical backing rather than vibes. Read tool: `tools/cycle_stats.py`. Field list and value vocabularies: the relocated-reference note. Adding a `cycle_shape` or `outcome_class` value requires an amendment to this ADR.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Single source of truth for "what decisions have been made and why" across the hub.
- Co-location with `rulebook/principles.md` means the upward justification (P3) is verifiable without leaving the repo.
- JSONL is grep-able and stream-able; weekly sharding keeps individual files manageable.
- `cost_usd` field reserved means we don't re-cut the schema when B2 lands.
- Role-id `agent` values publish without a persona scrub.

**Negative:**
- Manual entry writing is friction. Mitigation: workers emit a DECISIONS section that the orchestrator lifts into ledger format with minimal transformation.
- Append-only means correcting structural mistakes requires supersedure entries, which adds noise. Acceptable: the noise IS the audit value.
- Entries written before 2026-10-08 use older `id`, `ts` and `agent` conventions. Readers map them; they are never rewritten.

**Open questions deferred to follow-up ADRs:**
- Cost-tracking mechanism (B2). Once the source is identified, `cost_usd` gets populated retroactively for entries within the relevant window.
- Whether project orchestrators write to `pantheon-config/ledger/` directly or to per-project ledgers that get periodically merged. v0 assumes single ledger; revisit when the second project comes online.
