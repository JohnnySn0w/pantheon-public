## Why this ADR exists

Pantheon is building **A2A (Agent-to-Agent) federation** — our seed talking to other seeds over an open standard (T-1882, operator-ratified TG 865). Rung 1 (our own seats, loopback) is built and proven; rung 3 is an **external node — "a second Pantheon seed we stand up ourselves before any stranger."** Rung 1 deliberately shipped with **no auth** ("fake auth on 127.0.0.1 tests nothing") and *parked* the real identity layer. This ADR is that parked layer: **how a seed proves who it is to another seed whose implementation we do not control** — the operator's "passport."

The naive answer ("maximally decentralized identity → DIDs everywhere") is wrong, and the T-2015 reviewer pass caught why: "regardless of implementation" has two opposite senses, and a verifiable-credential-over-A2A passport is the *least* interoperable choice for an arbitrary counterparty (a Copilot Studio or Bedrock agent will not verify a Pantheon VC). The resolution is the operator's TG-4114 synthesis, recorded here as the decision.

## Context — what is already fixed (do not design over it)

1. **A2A supplies the envelope, not the identity.** A2A (donated to the Linux Foundation, June 2025) has each Agent Card *advertise* supported schemes under `securitySchemes` — the OpenAPI set: **API key, HTTP Bearer/Basic, OAuth2, OpenID Connect, mTLS**. Clients fetch the (unauthenticated) card and initiate the advertised flow. OAuth2/OIDC/mTLS/API-key are **A2A-native — every conformant agent speaks them off the shelf**; the *trust model* is left to us.
2. **The card-auth primitive is already chosen — the operator's TG-869 ruling** (recorded in the rung-1 design as the federation-port spec): `SRV` endpoint pointing, a **DNSSEC-signed `TXT` carrying the agent-card URL + card HASH** (tamper-evident pinning "without registries or `did:wba`"), and **a manual allowlist of known seed cards** — with `did:wba`/decentralized-registries **explicitly punted as "genuinely unsettled."** This authenticates **who the endpoint IS**. The passport authenticates **who is CONNECTING**. They are orthogonal axes of mutual auth, and this ADR **composes with** that primitive — it does not reverse it.
3. **Rung-3 public exposure is operator-gated.** Publishing a public Agent Card / DID document is when Pantheon becomes externally addressable; it couples to the T-1790-class ingress question and stays the operator's sign-off.

## Decision

### D1 — Card-auth and presenter-auth compose, in that ORDER
A seed connection is mutually authenticated on two orthogonal axes: **(a) card-auth** — is this endpoint the seed it claims to be? — via the ratified DNSSEC card-hash pin + allowlist (TG-869); and **(b) presenter-auth** — the passport — is the party connecting who they claim to be? The order is load-bearing: **a party verifies the endpoint (a) BEFORE presenting its own identity (b) to it.** This is precisely why rung 1 came first and correctly parked (b). The passport is therefore the **named next artifact on a ratified arc, not a reversal of the TG-869 `did:wba` punt** (see D3 for why the punt stays honored).

**Handshake state machine (NORMATIVE — folded from Codex ORG-041, defect 2):** the compose-order is only a guarantee if it is *enforceable*, so both sides run this ordered, fail-closed sequence and MUST NOT proceed on a skipped or degraded step:
1. **Resolve endpoint (card-auth).** Fetch the DNSSEC-signed `TXT` (SRV-pointed), then the Agent Card at the advertised URL. **Fail-closed:** absent/invalid DNSSEC chain → ABORT (never fall back to unsigned DNS); a card whose hash ≠ the `TXT`-pinned hash → ABORT; an endpoint not in the local allowlist → ABORT. No step is skippable "for now."
2. **Select scheme.** From the intersection of the counterparty card's advertised `securitySchemes` and our supported adapters, pick the **strongest common** scheme by a fixed precedence — **mTLS > OIDC/Bearer > API-key** (VC offered only to capability-kin, D3). **Downgrade rejection:** if a stronger scheme was advertised on a prior handshake with this endpoint and is now absent, treat it as suspicious → ABORT rather than silently step down; **no common scheme → ABORT** (never present an identity over a scheme we cannot have it verified in).
3. **Present identity (presenter-auth).** Only after 1–2 succeed does a party present its passport, translated into the selected scheme.
4. **Admit as UNTRUSTED content (D4).** A verified identity gates *who*, never *what*: the admitted session's payloads stay untrusted regardless of how strong the auth was.

There is **no partial-trust state** — a handshake is fully mutually-authenticated or it is refused; each ABORT logs the failing precondition. This state machine is the enforceable form of D1's ordering claim, not a separate build concern.

### D2 — The passport is ONE canonical seed identity: `did:web:your-domain.example`
A seed has a single stable identity — a **DID**, anchored as `did:web` at a domain it controls: for our seed, `did:web:your-domain.example`, resolving to a DID document at `https://your-domain.example/.well-known/did.json`. The DID document publishes the seed's verification keys and does not change per counterparty. Seats receive credentials chained to the seed DID (seed→seat delegation), matching the rung-1 **per-seat, not per-seed** scoping.
**Arc synergy — and an honest build-cost (orchestrator review 2026-07-24):** the anchor lives on the *same* `your-domain.example` zone + Cloudflare/caddy substrate we just brought up for the git primary (ORG-046 / T-2007 / T-2014) — that coherence is real. But it is **not** "a file that already exists": `did:web:your-domain.example` resolves at the **apex** `your-domain.example/.well-known/did.json`, which is a **different vhost from `git.your-self-hosted-git.example`** and is **not served today** (the apex has no A record — verified 000). So step 1 of the build (D6) is a genuine, if small, task: add the apex A record (via our Cloudflare control) + a caddy vhost serving `/.well-known/did.json`. Note the anchor must be **anonymously resolvable** — it cannot live behind the `REQUIRE_SIGNIN_VIEW=true` posture set on `git.your-self-hosted-git.example`, which is a second reason to anchor at the apex rather than reuse the login-gated git subdomain.

**Key-purpose scoping (NORMATIVE — folded from Codex ORG-041, defect 1):** one DID backing three schemes is safe *only* if its `verificationMethod` entries are purpose-partitioned, never one reused key. The DID document MUST: bind each key to its DID-core **verification relationship** — `authentication` for the presenter-proof keys (the mTLS-bound x509 key and the JWT-signing key), `assertionMethod` for the VC-issuance key, `keyAgreement` only if a scheme ever needs it; give each key a **distinct `kid`**; carry a **per-scheme algorithm allowlist** (e.g. the mTLS key is used for TLS only, the JWT key for JWS only, each pinned to its algorithm) so a key minted for one protocol cannot be presented into another; and enforce **no key reuse across schemes**. This closes the cross-protocol key/alg-confusion surface that a single one-root-for-everything key otherwise opens — the price of the one-DID convenience is paid here, in the document's structure. **DID-Core caveat (folded from Codex second-opinion, 2026-08-08):** the per-scheme *algorithm allowlist* is **not a standard DID-Core `verificationMethod` field** — a `did:web` document has no normative slot for it, so a non-Pantheon resolver will ignore it. It is therefore an **intentional local annotation, and the enforcement lives verifier-side**: our adapters pin each key to its protocol+algorithm and reject a cross-protocol presentation regardless of what any DID document declares. Expressing it in our own `did.json` documents that policy; it is never the mechanism that enforces it — a public DID field cannot bind a hostile presenter. (The verification-relationship partitioning + distinct `kid` above *are* DID-Core standard and stay in the document as written.)

### D3 — Multilingual presentation: the same identity, translated into the counterparty's scheme
The passport is presented in **whatever scheme the counterparty's Agent Card `securitySchemes` demands**, all backed by the one DID. The enabling fact: **a single `did:web` document holds multiple `verificationMethod` entries** (a JWT-signing JWK, an x509/mTLS-bound key, a VC-issuance key). So:
- counterparty wants **mTLS** → a client cert bound to a DID key;
- counterparty wants a **bearer token** → this splits into **two distinct adapters the ADR names separately (folded from Codex ORG-041, defect 4), because conflating them oversells the IdP-decoupling claim:** **(a) an HTTP-Bearer profile** — a self-contained JWT signed under a DID `authentication` key, verified by the counterparty resolving our `did:web` document; this is what "IdP-agnostic" *actually* delivers (no issuer, no IdP, no shared anchor), and it is an **HTTP Bearer scheme, NOT OIDC**. **(b) true OIDC/OAuth2** — required only when a counterparty will accept nothing less than conformant OIDC: it needs issuer metadata (`/.well-known/openid-configuration`), `aud`/`iss` validation, a JWKS-or-DID-resolution key mapping, and token-issuance semantics — i.e. real IdP machinery (Zitadel, the ADR-225 Echo-IdP as ONE option, not a competing choice). **The IdP-decoupling claim holds for (a), not (b):** we present (a) by default and stand up (b)'s IdP only for a counterparty that demands it. Either way a seed is not an Echo host (D2 chains seats to the seed DID, not to any IdP); the two arcs stay decoupled.
- counterparty wants a **VC / VP** → a verifiable presentation issued by the DID (**kin only** — see the kin definition below);
- counterparty wants an **API key** → a registered per-counterparty key mapped to the passport. **NOT a DID-backed cryptographic proof (folded from Codex second-opinion, 2026-08-08):** an API key is a **bearer secret whose association to our DID is a side-channel lookup mapping, not a cryptographic binding** — presenting the key proves possession of the secret, never control of `did:web:your-domain.example`. It is admitted only through an **explicit enrollment step** (we mint + register the per-counterparty key out of band and record its binding to the passport on our side), and it carries the **weakest trust weight** — consistent with its last place in the D1 precedence (`mTLS > OIDC/Bearer > API-key`). So the "one DID backs every scheme" claim is **literal + cryptographic for mTLS / Bearer / VC, and associative-only for API-key**; the ADR states this rather than letting the shared-identity framing over-sell the API-key path.

**Definition — "kin" (capability, not ownership):** a **kin** counterparty is one that runs compatible tooling and can therefore mutually verify our DID/VC, *regardless of who operates it.* This is distinct from "a seed WE stand up" (ownership). The VC adapter is kin-only on the **capability** sense: only a counterparty able to resolve+verify a DID/VP should be offered that scheme. The first rung-3 target is *both* ours and kin, but the two diverge later — a friend running our stack is kin but not ours — and all adapter-selection logic rides on the capability boundary, not the ownership one.

A2A's card advertisement IS the negotiation surface: fetch the counterparty's card, pick the strongest scheme both support, present the one identity that way. **This is why the TG-869 punt stays honored:** the DID is an *internal anchor* and is only ever presented to a stranger *translated* into a scheme they natively verify (OIDC/mTLS) — interop is never bet on the unsettled decentralized-identity camp. "Multilingual for reach, one passport for identity; missing either loses."

### D4 — NORMATIVE: the passport authenticates IDENTITY, never confers CONTENT trust
A cryptographically **verified** kin seed gets **NO trust discount on its payload.** A counterparty fluent in our own ontology / principle-IDs is *more* persuasive, not less — and a passport makes that failure mode tempting. Inbound A2A Messages remain **untrusted content** (the Telegram-input discipline): the passport gates *who is speaking*; standing access-control still forbids the narrow skill surface from honoring any pairing / access-grant / action request regardless of who presents a valid passport. This is a **normative constraint of this ADR, not a footnote** — it is the exact invariant a federated identity layer endangers. (Carried verbatim from the rung-1 "kin-seed social-engineering note.")

### D5 — Seed signing-key custody, rotation, AND revocation are the crown jewel; decide before the first handshake
With one DID backing every presentation, the seed's signing key is single-point-of-total-compromise: its loss = full seed impersonation across every seat and every scheme. Three surfaces, all required parts of the first build, load-bearing for the *first kin* handshake — not deferrable to "before non-kin":
- **Custody + rotation** of the seed signing key — offline root + online delegated keys is the recommended shape; what may sign with it, and how the root rotates. **Seed-root compromise recovery (folded from Codex ORG-041, defect 3):** root compromise is the total-impersonation event, so its recovery path is *specified, not assumed* — rotate the root offline, publish the new key in the `did:web` document (which is the single propagation point: counterparties re-resolve it on their next handshake), and revoke every delegated key chained to the old root. The recovery latency is bounded by the counterparty's DID-document cache TTL, so `did.json` is served with a **short `Cache-Control`** — a rotation must propagate in minutes, not days.
- **Seat-credential revocation** — **short-lived delegated seat credentials** with a **concrete TTL (recommended ≤ 24h), re-minted on a cadence well inside the TTL** so revocation is expiry-by-default. For **sub-TTL** revocation (a seat compromised inside its validity window) an explicit **deny path**: the verifier checks a published deny-list, fetched/cached with a short TTL that bounds worst-case exposure between a revoke and its enforcement; a seat presenting a denied credential is refused even while the credential is still time-valid.
- **Counterparty de-authorization** — a rogue *counterparty* is removed via the **TG-869 allowlist** (removing its card/cert entry is the card-level revocation surface we already have). **Propagation:** each seed re-reads its *own* allowlist per handshake, so removal takes effect on that counterparty's next connection attempt — de-auth is **local to each seed's allowlist, with no cross-seed gossip**, matching the deliberately-manual allowlist model. The card-auth and presenter-auth layers each carry their own revocation, matching D1's two axes.
- **VC status / revocation (folded from Codex ORG-041, defect 3):** issued VCs are **NOT fire-and-forget** — each carries a **status mechanism** (a StatusList2021-style revocation list referenced via the credential's `credentialStatus`, hosted on the same anon-resolvable apex as the DID document) so a kin counterparty can confirm a presented VC has not been revoked before honoring it.

This ADR does not hand-wave any of these surfaces; §Open-decisions surfaces the custody-model choice and the two load-bearing revocation parameters (seat-cred TTL, VC status-list mechanism) for the operator's granularity call at ratify.

### D6 — Build order: minimum-viable first, adapters additive
Ship, in order: **(1)** stand up the DID anchor — add the apex `your-domain.example` A record (via our Cloudflare control) + a caddy vhost serving `/.well-known/did.json` (a small but real build step per D2, NOT a pre-existing file), then publish the `did:web:your-domain.example` document — cheap and low-regret, independent of everything else; **(2)** the **mTLS adapter for the first kin seed** (composes directly with the card-hash + allowlist); this is sufficient for rung 3's named counterparty. **(3)** the OIDC adapter (stranger reach) and **(4)** the VC adapter (kin self-sovereign delegation) are **additive and independent**, built when a counterparty needs them — not speculatively.

### Security considerations — folded clarifications (T-2803 triage, 2026-08-10)

Per the operator's ORG-047 walk (2026-08-10), the primary/secondary security considerations were triaged (`notes/2026-08-10-org047-security-considerations-triage.md`). Narrower concerns were split into **referenced ADRs** — provider-neutral network capabilities → **ORG-071**; lifecycle health/recovery → **ORG-072**; content/tool authorization → **ORG-069**; evidence & governance → **ORG-070**; credential + key lifecycle → **Split A** (ticket T-2811, unauthored, gated on the value-decisions below). Replay/freshness, clock-skew, and disposable/non-Pantheon interop tests are routed to the **T-2018** build. The remaining six fold here as clarifications of the decisions above:

- **Bootstrap trust (clarifies D1/D5):** first contact is admitted only by **manual, out-of-band** entry into the TG-869 allowlist — never auto-enrollment on passport strength. The approval-workflow rigor around that entry lives in ORG-070 (governance).
- **Versioning (clarifies D1):** each endpoint's advertised scheme/version is pinned per-counterparty; an **unexpected version change is treated like a downgrade** — re-verify or ABORT, never silently accept. (Downgrade itself is already normative in D1 step 2.)
- **Seat isolation / blast-radius (clarifies D2):** each seat holds an **independently-revocable** delegated credential and can never present as another seat, so a seat compromise's blast-radius is **exactly one seat**. OS/uid-level worker isolation is T-1347's domain, not this arc.
- **Provider portability (D3 — the thesis):** portability is not a separate concern but the ORG-047 thesis itself (one DID, multilingual adapters); recorded as covered, pointer only.
- **Minimization (clarifies D3):** minimal disclosure — a party presents **only** the credential the demanded scheme needs; a seat credential discloses the **seat's** authority, never the seed's full seat-roster.
- **Observability (clarifies D1 → ORG-070):** every handshake **outcome** (admit or abort, plus the failing precondition on abort) is logged and metered (scheme-selection, abort-rate); observability depth lives in ORG-070.

The three **value-decisions** these considerations surfaced — seat-credential TTL (rec ≤ 24h), VC revocation via StatusList2021, and the key-custody envelope (operator-reserved, secret-bearing) — are tracked in **T-2810** for the operator's call and gate Split A; they do not block this ADR's shape.

## Alternatives considered (and why the layering supersedes them)

Picking a **single** scheme was the first-draft framing; the T-2015 reviewer showed it narrows the goal:
- **OIDC/OpenID-Federation alone** — A2A-native and mature, but requires every seed to run an IdP and still needs a shared trust anchor (a governance question). *Retained as the stranger-facing adapter (D3), not the whole answer.*
- **DID/VC alone** — self-issued and structure-fitting, but a VC is the *least* interoperable choice for a non-Pantheon A2A endpoint, and "self-sovereign" oversells `did:web` (it roots in DNS + Web PKI — "no *new* anchor," not "no anchor"). *Retained as the identity core + the kin adapter (D2/D3).*
- **mTLS + allowlist alone** — complete and standard for kin, but the hand-managed allowlist does not scale to open/many-party federation. *Retained as the first adapter + composes with card-auth (D6).*

The layering (D1–D3) keeps each option's strength and discards the "pick one and lose the others" cost: identity is unified (one DID), reach is universal (speak every scheme), and no interop is bet on unsettled tech.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** one identity, universal reach; reuses hardened `your-domain.example` substrate; composes with (does not reverse) the ratified TG-869 primitive; the Echo/Zitadel IdP work is reused as an adapter, not stranded; adapters are independently shippable, so rung-3-for-kin is a small first build.
- **Cost / risk:** each adapter is real engineering (the DID doc is cheap; the adapters are not); the seed signing key is a new crown-jewel custody obligation (D5); a two-sided build for the VC path (the counterparty must verify DIDs — fine for kin, which is why VC is kin-only).
- **Reversible?** The DID stub and the mTLS adapter are additive and removable; nothing here is a one-way door until a public Agent Card is published (operator-gated, D-context-3).

## DECISIONS (justification)

- **Composes-not-reverses (D1)** — justified by the rung-ladder's own sequencing (verify-endpoint-before-present-identity) and by keeping the operator's TG-869 card-auth primitive intact; the passport is the arc's parked next step, satisfying **P1** (structural answer on a principle-touching design, no expedient interim).
- **One-identity-many-adapters (D2/D3)** — governed by **P1 (Architecture over policy):** making the auth scheme a *structural adapter boundary* (negotiated per-counterparty) rather than a hardcoded/policy dependency is the P1 move. **ORG-031 is the PRECEDENT, not the authority** — the same swappable-boundary discipline, previously applied to the harness boundary; "justified by ORG-031" would overstate its reach (it governs the harness boundary specifically), so it is cited as prior application, not warrant. Shares the identity-not-IP framing with **ORG-046** (bind to an identity that belongs to the seed, presented over whatever transport/scheme). *(Rulebook check, orchestrator 2026-07-24: there is no distinct anti-lock-in/provider-independence principle P1–P7 — P1 is the correct governing id.)*
- **Untrusted-content invariant (D4)** — justified by the ratified rung-1 security posture (OWASP LLM01/06; inbound-A2A-as-untrusted-content), carried forward as normative.
- **Anti-lock-in + security-as-baseline** — *design values* carried from ADR-225's "principles-in-play" framing (not rulebook P-principles): the identity layer must be cheap enough for a one-person seed yet interoperate with an enterprise's IdP unchanged; multilingual presentation is the anti-lock-in cash-out, and it ladders under P1 above.
